# Trade Warehousing and Freight

**Direction:** goods stay physically located in planetary warehouses or government-owned station warehouses. Corporations set prices. Buyers can collect, sellers can deliver, or carriers can fulfill freight contracts. Space warehousing is a business opportunity.

Separate warehouse ownership, cargo ownership, operator permissions, and carrier custody. Corporate stock in a public warehouse remains corporate unless sold. An inventory ledger references actual stock; it cannot conjure a duplicate shipment.

Proposed orders reserve deposited goods and buyer payment. Terms include item/quality, quantity, price, pickup/destination, deadline, and loss liability. Settlement occurs once at the agreed handover. Define cancellations, partial deliveries, and missing destinations before implementation.

Storage is finite. Warehouse jobs include construction, operation, leasing bays, and staging projects. Fees and service targets are open. Repeatedly shuffling the same goods must not manufacture handling rewards.

Government transport takes passengers with bounded baggage and an assignment-equipment allowance (proposal). Bulk equipment and commercial cargo should create freight demand. Physical cross-force loading/unloading is an early technical experiment, not an assumed native feature.

Initial trade prototype: solid items, one quality tier, deposited stock, one pickup transaction. Fluids, quality, spoilage, and interplanetary custody expand later.
